**Method for mouthpart (trophi) examination**

1. Transfer the rotifer using a small bore glass pipette to the center of a microscope slide. Minimize the liquid associated with the transfer.
1. Place a drop of fresh bleach on the specimen and gently drop a coverslip over the specimen.
1. Locate the specimen on the slide while viewing through low magnification of the microscope. Once located keep the rotifer in view until the bleach completely dissolves the inner tissues. In most cases the rotifer will swell and the mouthparts will be dispelled from the surrounding lorica.
1. The mouthparts can be manipulated by taking advantage of the surface tension between the two glass surfaces. Gently nudge the edge of the coverslip with the tip of pencil or dissecting needle. If there is too much liquid on the slide the mouthpart will be difficult to maneuver. This can rectified using a small amount of tissue paper twisted into a fine point. Position the tip of the tissue paper at the edge of the coverslip to draw off any excess liquid.
1. View the mouthparts using 400X or greater magnification. Because the mouthparts have 3-dimensional structure they must be oriented in a plane that can be compared to reference drawings or images.
