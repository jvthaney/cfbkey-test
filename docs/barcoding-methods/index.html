Barcoding Methods

Methods for Evaluating the 28S rDNA D3 Expansion Segment as a Genetic Barcode for Freshwater Calanoid and Cyclopoid Copepods

Specimens were collected using vertical tow nets from lakes throughout New England by the University of New Hampshire 's Center for Freshwater Biology (UNH CFB) and the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) Region 1. Each individual adult copepod was identified morphologically and digital vouchers were taken of the whole specimen and all anatomical structures important to its species identification. DNA was extracted from each individual using chelating resin, amplified by polymerase chain reaction (PCR) using primers designed for the D3 expansion segment (Litvaitis et al 1994). The amplified DNA region was cycle sequenced commercially. The sequences were aligned by ClustalW method. Pairwise distances and Neighbor-joining tree analyzes were used to evaluate the ability of the barcode to correctly assign individuals to species.
